## 6. Lifecycle-state comparability boundary

The R2 and P-A deadline models expose the same lifecycle states: `Scheduled`, `CancelPending`, `Cancelled`, `Claimed`, `Dispatched`, `Completed` and `Failed`.

P-D now exposes `Scheduled`, `CancelPending`, `Cancelled`, `Superseded`, `Claimed`, `Dispatched` and `Completed`. The comparable lifecycle path is `close_watermark() -> Claimed -> dispatch_event() -> Dispatched -> complete() -> Completed`. Dispatch requires a closed watermark covering the event; repeated dispatch in `Dispatched` or `Completed` is idempotent. Completion before dispatch is rejected. A dispatched generation cannot be replaced until it reaches `Completed`; an older cancelled/replaced generation returns `StaleIgnored` at dispatch.

The exploratory `claim_next()` primitive still can claim without closing W. That result is deliberately candidate-local: `dispatch_event()` rejects such a claim with `InvariantViolation`, and `complete()` rejects it. Do not count `claim_next()` as the R2/P-A logical claim operation; the comparable claim is performed atomically by `close_watermark()`.

P-D does not expose a `Failed` deadline status. R2 and P-A do expose `Failed` as a terminal deadline status and include it in the closed-watermark validator, but their W05 deadline APIs do not provide a transition that marks a deadline Failed. Partial-effect and recovery semantics belong to the separate ResourceLedger/transaction/snapshot contracts. Therefore P-D cannot mirror the preconstructed `Failed`-status validation row; do not add a bare enum value and claim end-to-end failure/recovery equivalence.

Classification:
- `Dispatched`: **DIRECTLY COMPARABLE for the scheduler lifecycle transition**. P-D now requires a closed watermark, transitions only `Claimed -> Dispatched`, and treats retries idempotently. This does not prove physical effect/partial-commit equivalence.
- `Failed`: **NOT REPRESENTED** in P-D. R2/P-A terminal failure semantics, including any pending subeffects, are not covered by P-D lifecycle equivalence.
- `Superseded`: **P-D-specific** terminal record. The low-level `publish()` primitive can create it when replacing an older scheduled event. It has no direct R2/P-A status counterpart and must not silently be equated with `Cancelled`.
- Contextual publication rejects a successor while the prior generation remains `Scheduled`, `CancelPending`, `Claimed` or `Dispatched`; after `Completed`, a higher generation is admitted. The context-aware replacement operation remains the comparable path for cancellation/arbitration/republication.

These are prototype representation limits, not claims about physical SMS hardware. Whether P-D needs explicit dispatch/failure stages remains an R3 semantic comparison question; no production CHRONOS decision is implied.
